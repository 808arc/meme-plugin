## Humor, memory and learning

### The exception of humour: Iconicity, Phonemic Surprisal, Memory Recall, and Emotional Associations

- [ ] Verify
- **Citation:** Kilpatrick, A. & Flaksman, M. (2025). arXiv:2502.01682.
- **Link:** [arxiv.org/abs/2502.01682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01682)
- **Key finding:** humorous words are positive yet show higher surprisal and memorability. Described as a meta-study building on existing findings. "No new experiment" is not stated in the abstract.
- **Implication for this plugin:** humor works as memory aid, not only entertainment. Surprise may be part of the mechanism: a caption twisting the phrasing may beat one restating the fact.

### A SMART Mnemonic Sounds like "Glue Tonic": Mixing LLMs with Student Feedback to Make Mnemonic Learning Stick

- [ ] Verify
- **Citation:** Balepur, N. et al. (2024). arXiv:2406.15352. EMNLP 2024.
- **Link:** [arxiv.org/abs/2406.15352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15352)
- **Key finding:** expressed and observed preferences disagree; what students find helpful does not always match what helps. 2,684 preferences from 45 students.
- **Implication for this plugin:** user liking a caption is not enough. Measure recall too. Methodological template for an LLM memory aid.

### Humor in Software Testing Education

- [ ] Verify
- **Citation:** Graßl, I. & Baudry, B. (2026). arXiv:2606.21682.
- **Link:** [arxiv.org/abs/2606.21682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21682)
- **Key finding:** measures engagement, belonging and creative thinking. Not retention. Percentages reported in earlier notes (65.5%, 58.6%, 46.6%) are NOT in the abstract; full text unreadable in this check.
- **Implication for this plugin:** memes may ease stress on hard questions. Not evidence for memory gain.

### The Resiliency of Memorability: A Predictor of Memory Separate from Attention and Priming

- [ ] Verify
- **Citation:** Bainbridge, W. A. (2017). arXiv:1703.07738.
- **Link:** [arxiv.org/abs/1703.07738](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07738)
- **Key finding:** memorability is an intrinsic, independent attribute of an image.
- **Implication for this plugin:** image choice matters apart from caption. Favor memorable images in selection, alongside the reverse-spaced-repetition rule.

### KoroT-3E: A Personalized Musical Mnemonics Tool for Enhancing Memory Retention of Complex Computer Science Concepts

- [ ] Verify
- **Citation:** arXiv:2409.10446 (2024).
- **Link:** [arxiv.org/abs/2409.10446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10446)
- **Key finding:** personalized musical mnemonic tool for CS concepts, aimed at retention. Not checked against the abstract yet.
- **Implication for this plugin:** comparable generated-mnemonic tool. Check how it evaluates retention.

### Enhancing Student Learning with LLM-Generated Retrieval Practice Questions: An Empirical Study in Data Science Courses

- [ ] Verify
- **Citation:** An, Y. (2025). arXiv:2507.05629.
- **Link:** [arxiv.org/abs/2507.05629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05629)
- **Key finding:** students with LLM-generated retrieval practice reached 89% accuracy on retention, against 73% in the week without it.
- **Implication for this plugin:** supports the rule that the meme waits until the user tries the question.

## How memes spread and persuade

### Strong Men Create Good Memes: Far-Right Memes as Means of Authoritarian Legitimation in Russia

- [ ] Verify
- **Citation:** Smirnova, J. (2026). arXiv:2601.14190.
- **Link:** [arxiv.org/abs/2601.14190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14190)
- **Key finding:** far-right memes co-produce state propaganda through bottom-up narratives of legitimation. Humor is NOT in the abstract; earlier claim removed.
- **Implication for this plugin:** memes can carry state narratives. Facts in filled slots must be checked against the question (#7).

### Narratives of War: Ukrainian Memetic Warfare on Twitter

- [ ] Verify
- **Citation:** Mejova, Y. et al. (2023). arXiv:2309.08363.
- **Link:** [arxiv.org/abs/2309.08363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08363)
- **Key finding:** victim narratives get about twice the retweets. Hostile enemy narratives resonate more in countries giving more aid. Audience-sharing claim is NOT in the abstract.
- **Implication for this plugin:** narrative framing changes spread. Prefer neutral framing for learning content.

### Detecting Propaganda Techniques in Memes

- [ ] Verify
- **Citation:** Dimitrov, D. et al. (2021). arXiv:2109.08013.
- **Link:** [arxiv.org/abs/2109.08013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08013)
- **Key finding:** corpus of 950 memes, annotated with 22 propaganda techniques. Confirmed in abstract.
- **Implication for this plugin:** caption must match image. Template extraction keeps the image link.

### SemEval-2021 Task 6: Detection of Persuasion Techniques in Texts and Images

### On the Origins of Memes by Means of Fringe Web Communities

- [ ] Verify

- **Link:** [arxiv.org/abs/1805.12512](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12512)

### Virality Prediction and Community Structure in Social Networks

## Seed list from issue #2 (journal articles, not on arXiv)

Record checked against abstracts and listings. Full texts not read yet.

- [ ] Schmidt (1994). "Effects of humor on sentence memory." *JEP: Learning, Memory, and Cognition*, 20(4), 953–967.
  - **Key finding:** humorous sentences recalled better on free and cued recall; effect weaker under incidental learning.
  - **Implication for this plugin:** humor aids recall of the sentence it sits on. Supports humor on the fact, not beside it.
- [ ] Banas, Dunbar, Rodriguez & Liu (2011). "A Review of Humor in Educational Settings: Four Decades of Research." *Communication Education*, 60(1), 115–144. [doi.org/10.1080/03634523.2010.496867](https://doi.org/10.1080/03634523.2010.496867)
  - **Key finding:** review of humor's functions and theories in teaching; explains inconsistent findings.
  - **Implication for this plugin:** results vary by setting. Test memes in the plugin's own sessions (#5).
- [ ] Kaplan & Pascoe (1977). "Humorous Lectures and Humorous Examples: Some Effects upon Comprehension and Retention." *JEP*, 69(1), 61–65. [doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.69.1.61](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.69.1.61)
  - **Key finding:** N = 508. Humor did not improve immediate comprehension. Six-week retention was better only for humor tied to the lecture's concepts.
  - **Implication for this plugin:** humor must relate to the concept. Generic jokes may not help memory.
- [ ] McDaniel & Einstein (1986). "Bizarre Imagery as an Effective Memory Aid: The Importance of Distinctiveness." *JEP: LMC*, 12(1), 54–65.
  - **Key finding:** bizarre items recalled better when mixed with common ones; no benefit when studied in separate lists. Distinctiveness, not attention, is the favoured account.
  - **Implication for this plugin:** a meme stands out only against ordinary material. Keep memes rare, not constant.
- [ ] Testing effect. Roediger & Karpicke (2006). "Test-enhanced learning." *Psychological Science*, 17(3), 249–255. Review: Rowland (2014). *Psychological Bulletin*, 140(6), 1432–1463. [doi.org/10.1037/a0037559](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559)
  - **Key finding:** testing beats restudy; meta-analysis effect around g = 0.50; initial recall tests help most.
  - **Implication for this plugin:** supports "reveal the question first". The learner tries before the meme shows.
